# Brain dump

This is the single strongest way to pay down **thinking debt**. Every loop the head is holding — "remember to…", "I should…", "what if…" — keeps drawing on the thinking budget until it's written down somewhere trusted. Nothing gets solved during a dump; the only job is **capture**.

Load the Clear Head config: `~/.clear-head/config.md`, a "Clear Head config" document in the person's connected notes or docs, or the project instructions. Use it for the inbox location and personal triggers. If it's missing, capture into the conversation and offer clear-head-setup at the end.

## Capture rules

- One item per line, in the person's own words. Half-thoughts, worries and vague "should look into…" items all count.
- Stay a scribe: no advice, no solving, no rewording, no judging the size of the list. Ask a question only to split two things that arrived as one.
- Keep the pace light. Short acknowledgements ("got it", "next") keep the person in flow.
- A list that feels big or a little uncomfortable is normal: that's the debt becoming visible, not growing. Say this once if the person seems uneasy.

## Full dump (10–20 minutes)

1. **Open.** One line on what's about to happen, then: "What's on your mind right now? Anything at all." Capture until it slows, asking "what else?" between bursts.
2. **Trigger walk.** Go through [triggers.md](triggers.md) one area at a time, plus the personal triggers from the config. Offer a few triggers per turn and capture whatever they set off. Move on from areas that don't fire; the person can say "skip" or "enough" any time.
3. **Quiet pause.** Ask the person to sit for a minute or two without doing anything, then ask what else surfaced. Things often arrive here.
4. **Save.** Show the full list with a count, then append it to the inbox with today's date (after confirmation if it's an external tool).
5. **Close.** Offer to run process-inbox now or later. Remind them the items are safe where they are; being written down is what lets the head let go, whether or not anything gets done today.

Done when the person has nothing more after the quiet pause and every item is saved to the inbox.

## Evening unload (5 minutes)

For the end of the day or before sleep.

1. Ask for three slow breaths.
2. Replay the day backwards from now: "What were you doing just before this? And before that?" Keep stepping back until the morning.
3. Capture anything that tugs: an unanswered message, a promise, a worry, an idea.
4. Save to the inbox. Leave processing for tomorrow's daily review.

## Quick capture

When the person just tells you something to remember mid-conversation, append it to the inbox with today's date and confirm in one line.

## When it's more than overload

If what surfaces sounds like real distress — hopelessness, crisis, thoughts of harm — set the list aside and respond as a caring person would. Encourage them to reach out to someone they trust or to a professional or local helpline. Clear Head is a productivity tool and offers no medical or mental-health advice.
