# Monthly reflection

The daily and weekly reviews keep the list working. Once a month, step back to ask whether it's working *for the person*: what actually got done, which tasks quietly dodge every review, and whether the month went to the things that matter most.

Load the Clear Head config: `~/.clear-head/config.md`, a "Clear Head config" document in the person's connected notes or docs, or the project instructions. Use it for tools, the concept mapping and working rules. If it's missing, offer clear-head-setup, or ask the person to paste their list and last month's completed tasks, and continue.

## Steps

1. **Load.** Pull:
   - the month's completed tasks (from the tool's history, or the journal)
   - every open task with its added date and due date
   - this month's journal, especially the `moved:` lines
   - last month's reflection, for the priorities noted then
2. **What got done.** Summarise by project or area of life and ask what the person is glad about. Keep it warm and brief, and don't score anything. Mention areas that got nothing this month (all work, no health, for instance) as an observation, not a judgement.
3. **Slippers.** These are tasks moved three or more times, open for more than a month, or overdue again and again. Line them up and, for each one, find out honestly *why* it slips:
   - **foggy**: no real first step. Find the first move with the task-rewriter rules.
   - **too big**: really a project. Make it one, with a single first step.
   - **not really wanted**, or someone else's want: run so-that-what, then drop it or renegotiate it.
   - **waiting on someone**: turn it into a follow-up task.
   - **dreaded**: shrink the first step until it's almost silly, or give it a fixed slot.

   Decide each one with the person.
4. **Fog sweep.** Read the rest of the list for fog, and find the first move in every vague task the slippers didn't already cover.
5. **Ideas box.** Run the ideas-box review, unless the person reviews ideas on a different rhythm.
6. **Life priorities.** Ask one question at a time, slowly:
   - "What matters most to you right now, across work, health, people, home, growth and fun?"
   - "Looking at the month, where did your time and attention actually go?"
   - "Is there something you want that got nothing this month?" If nothing is being done toward it, nothing will happen, so add one small first step or park it on purpose.
   - "Is there something you're still doing that no longer matters to you?"
   - "What would you like next month to be about?"

   Reflect back what you hear in the person's words. The so-that-what down direction helps link wants to tasks.
7. **Write it down.** Apply the task changes after the person confirms. Append the reflection to the journal: highlights, slipper decisions, priorities and next month's focus. Next month's reflection starts from it.

Done when every slipper has a decision, every vague task has a first move or an open question, and the priorities note is in the journal.
