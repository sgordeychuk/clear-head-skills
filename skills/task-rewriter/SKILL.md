# Task rewriter

A vague task is thinking debt in disguise. The person reads it, the brain quietly asks "and what exactly do I do?", gets no answer, and slides to something easier — and the task keeps nagging anyway. Having a plan for how to *start* is what quiets an unfinished task, so the wording itself is the plan.

The target is a task your **autopilot** can run: readable half-asleep, startable within five seconds, with all the thinking already done.

## A clear task

1. **Starts with a concrete verb**: call, email, write, buy, book, open, print, pay, ask, read, draft, list, send.
2. **Is the very first physical step**, not the outcome. "Get the car fixed" is an outcome; "Call the garage on Elm St to book an inspection" is a step.
3. **Needs no further thinking** to start. Everything needed (who, which file, which number, where) is in the task or its description.
4. **Fits in about 30 minutes.** Bigger means it's a project, or the first step hasn't been found yet.

**Fog** is the signal that no step exists yet: think about, figure out, look into, deal with, sort out, work on, handle, plan, decide, check on, meet, find time for, wait for — and bare nouns like "Dentist", "Anna", "Taxes".

## Steps

1. **Diagnose** which rule fails.
2. **Find the step with the person.** The real first step lives in their knowledge, so ask one targeted question rather than inventing it:
   - "What's the very first thing you'd physically do — open what, call whom, write where?"
   - "What's stopping you from doing it right now?" The blocker is usually the real task.
   - "Who would know?" → "Ask Sam…"
   - For think/decide tasks: thinking means answering a specific question. "What's the first question you'd need answered?" Then make the task about getting that answer: "Write three options for…", "Ask Lee what the budget is for…", "Compare the two offers in a table".
   - For projects: "If you had 20 minutes right now and had to make progress, what would you do?" Push past "everything" or "I need a free afternoon" to one concrete move.
3. **Rewrite**, keeping the goal visible in parentheses: `Call the garage on Elm St to book an inspection (Get the car fixed)`. When that step is done, replace the front part with the next step; the goal stays.
4. **Route what isn't a task.** An outcome needing several steps goes to projects with just its first step in the task list. Something waiting on another person becomes a follow-up: "Ask Dana whether the quote is ready", with a date.
5. **Apply** after the person confirms.

For many tasks at once, use a table: original | what fails | rewrite (or the question you need answered). Group the questions so the person answers them in one go.

Done when every task passes all four rules or has an open question waiting on the person.

## Examples

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| Taxes | Find last year's tax return in the Documents folder and put it on the desktop (File taxes) |
| Think about the team offsite | Write three possible offsite dates and email them to Priya (Team offsite) |
| Website | Email Jo to ask for the hosting login (Relaunch website) |
| Deal with the leaking tap | Text the landlord a photo of the leaking kitchen tap |
| Wait for Mark's feedback | Ask Mark whether he's had a chance to read the proposal (due Thu) |
| Learn Spanish | Book a trial lesson on the language app for Saturday morning (Learn Spanish) |
